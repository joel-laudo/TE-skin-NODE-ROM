Skin-Growth NODE ROM — Model D mechanistic analysis

Model D's CNN growth pathway is genuinely used — and fully redundant with Model A's own information

Do Models A and D reach similar accuracy because explicit growth feedback is ignored, or because two different representations of the same coupled mechanics–growth state happen to carry overlapping information?

Answer: the latter. A clean H2 result — the CNN encodes growth, the NODE depends on it, and that dependency is on information Model A already has.

The central result

Median validation displacement RMSE across 185 held-out simulations, under six conditions. Model D's true CNN features, its live-reconstructed (Model‑A‑predicted) features, and Model A itself are statistically indistinguishable — while every degraded substitute is significantly worse, in clear dose-response order.

What this rules in and out

✓
H2 — Confirmed
The CNN learns and uses a real growth representation, but that representation is conditionally redundant with what Model A already has. Both architectures parameterize the same coupled trajectory, just through different routes.
✗
H1 — Ruled out
Not a case of the CNN carrying genuinely distinct information that Model A can't reconstruct — the substitution test shows it can, and it works.
✗
H3 — Ruled out
Not a case of the pathway being effectively ignored — every intervention, including realistic mismatched features, measurably and significantly hurts performance.

Interpretation. Models A and D reach comparable long-horizon accuracy not because growth feedback is architecturally irrelevant, but because they access substantially overlapping information about the same coupled mechanics–growth state through two different representations. Model A infers growth-relevant information implicitly from its mechanical trajectory and known simulation context; Model D receives an explicit, learned representation of the growth field — one that is itself reconstructible from what Model A already observes.

Physical relevance of an internal variable does not imply that giving a model explicit access to it will improve performance, when the variable's dynamically useful content is already recoverable from the retained state.

Caveats and scope
  • Evidence types are kept distinct throughout: the decoding/correlation analyses show growth information is recoverable from a variable, which is weaker evidence than the inference-intervention and functional-substitution results showing it is actually used (the strongest evidence type here); the Jacobian sensitivity analysis is local-sensitivity evidence only, corroborating but not decisive on its own.
  • Bootstrap confidence intervals resample over the 185 validation simulations (each already collapsed to one trajectory-level scalar), not over autocorrelated timesteps — this is what makes them valid despite the underlying rollouts being time series. The three shuffle conditions' CIs specifically describe a companion-averaged (20-repeat) effect, a narrower question than the other conditions'.
  • The qA probe used for substitution was trained once (fixed seed) on Model D's own on-policy rollout trajectories; a multi-seed robustness check was not performed in this pass.
  • Intermediate-activation maps, dose-response amplitude scaling, physically-targeted counterfactual swaps, and a cross-model (B/C) comparison were explicitly deferred as lower-priority follow-ons, per the analysis's own scoping.
  • A units-mismatch bug (normalized vs. raw-physical inputs to the substitution probe) was caught and fixed during development, verified via an independent spot-check before the final substitution result was trusted.
